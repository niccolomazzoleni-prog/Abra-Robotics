Financing a robot in 2026: capital allowances, R&D tax relief, Made Smarter and leasing

Updated 9 October 2026Abra Robotics
In brief: in the UK, the main tax reliefs for a business buying a robot in 2026 are capital allowances: full expensing (100% for companies on qualifying main-rate plant and machinery), the new 40% first-year allowance from 1 January 2026 and the Annual Investment Allowance, up to £1 million per accounting period. R&D tax relief and, in England, Made Smarter Adoption can support the project around the robot. No scheme is automatic: whether your robot qualifies is for your accountant or tax adviser to confirm.

"How much does the G1 cost?" is the second question. The first is often: can we claim tax relief on it? The answer depends on who is buying (company, sole trader or partnership, SME, university), how the robot is acquired (outright purchase, loan, leasing) and how the project is set up. Here you will find the schemes in force in October 2026, what they require and where to check them in official sources.

General information, not tax or legal advice. The rules change often: before ordering, check the official pages (gov.uk, the HMRC Capital Allowances Manual, madesmarter.uk) and ask your accountant to confirm. The links to official sources in this article and in the Sources section point to the texts of the Italian scheme, which we cite for comparison.

Capital allowances: how they work

The rules are summarised on GOV.UK and, for the 40% allowance, in the HMRC Capital Allowances Manual (CA23195A). For comparison, Italy introduced an enhanced depreciation scheme in 2026, set out in article 1, paragraphs 427-436, of Italian Law No. 199 of 30 December 2025 (the Italian 2026 Budget Law), with implementing rules in an Italian interministerial decree of 7 May 2026 and a summary on the official page of the Italian Ministry of Enterprises (MIMIT). The key points for the UK:

  • Full expensing: companies can claim 100% capital allowances on qualifying main-rate plant and machinery, such as a robot bought for the business.
  • 40% first-year allowance: from 1 January 2026, a new permanent allowance for new and unused main-rate plant and machinery, available to Corporation Tax and Income Tax payers. It mainly helps unincorporated businesses and assets bought for leasing; the conditions are in the HMRC Capital Allowances Manual (CA23195A).
  • Annual Investment Allowance: sole traders, partnerships whose members are all individuals and limited companies can deduct the full value of qualifying plant and machinery from their profits before tax, up to an AIA limit of £1 million per accounting period.
  • Timing: you claim the AIA in the period in which you bought the item, and companies under common control share one allowance.
  • Nature: capital allowances reduce taxable profits; they are not a cash grant. Which allowance fits depends on your business structure and the asset.

Robots made outside the EU

The descriptions of the UK schemes in this overview set no condition on where the equipment was made: what counts is the type of asset and how it is used. In Italy, the enhanced depreciation was first limited to goods made in the EU or EEA; article 7 of Italian Decree-Law No. 38 of 27 March 2026, converted by Law No. 88 of 22 May 2026, removed that condition with effect from 1 January 2026. For a Unitree robot, which is made in China, what matters is therefore whether it qualifies as plant and machinery and what you use it for. Confirm this with your accountant.

What it is worth: an example with hypothetical figures

A profitable company buys a robot for €50,000 (excluding VAT) for use in its business. If the robot qualifies as main-rate plant and machinery, full expensing lets the company claim 100% capital allowances on it, so the whole cost is deducted from taxable profits. A sole trader or a partnership of individuals would look at the AIA instead, within the £1 million limit. The tax saved depends on your tax rate, your profits over the years and the rules for the specific asset, so we do not put a figure on it: this example shows the mechanism, not an estimate for your business.

Claims and applications: what comes before the order

Some steps are best taken before you commit to the purchase. The Italian scheme, for comparison, requires an advance filing on the portal of the GSE, Italy's state energy services agency. In the UK, in order:

  1. Capital allowances: check with your accountant which allowance fits your business structure and the asset. The claim is made for the accounting period in which you buy the robot.
  2. R&D tax relief: if the robot is part of an R&D project, your adviser checks which project costs qualify and whether you must notify HMRC in advance of the claim.
  3. Made Smarter Adoption: if you are a manufacturer in England, contact the regional partner for the postcode of your main site and check what support is available before you order.

Conditions and amounts change: before each step, check the official page of the scheme.

Linking the robot to the project: what it means

This is the point that most often causes problems for anyone buying a robot to experiment with. Capital allowances concern plant and machinery used in the business; R&D tax relief concerns qualifying R&D work, for example on robot control, manipulation or embodied AI; Made Smarter Adoption supports technology adoption in manufacturing. A few practical pointers, always to be checked with your accountant or the programme:

  • A cobot such as the Fairino FR3 (from €5,448 excl. VAT) installed in a cell and connected to the MES is the simplest case to document as production equipment.
  • For humanoids and quadrupeds the version matters: EDU versions (for example the Go2 EDU or the G1 U series) have an open SDK and allow you to develop data exchange and your own software; the base versions designed for use with the app are harder to integrate.
  • A robot bought for demonstrations or trade fairs, with no role in a process or an R&D project, is hard to link to R&D tax relief or an adoption programme: ask your accountant how it is treated.

R&D tax relief: merged scheme and ERIS

R&D tax relief is the scheme for companies that develop something new around the robot, rather than simply buying it. Italy, for comparison, has a different model for SMEs, a state contribution to the interest on an investment loan, described on the Italian Ministry of Enterprises page on its SME equipment scheme. The key points of the UK scheme, according to GOV.UK:

  • it applies to accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, for companies doing qualifying R&D, for example on robot control, manipulation or embodied AI;
  • companies claim under the merged R&D expenditure credit, at 20% of qualifying expenditure;
  • loss-making R&D-intensive SMEs claim under enhanced R&D intensive support (ERIS) instead;
  • your adviser checks which project costs qualify and whether you must notify HMRC in advance of the claim.

If you are also considering capital allowances, check with your accountant how the two schemes combine on the same project.

Made Smarter Adoption (England)

Made Smarter Adoption is a programme for small and medium-sized manufacturing and engineering businesses with manufacturing premises in England, delivered by regional partners according to the postcode of your main site. Support includes impartial technology advice, digital road-mapping and leadership training, and in several regions match-funded grants of up to £20,000 for hardware or software. A cobot or a palletising system may fit an adoption project, but support varies by region: check availability with your regional partner on madesmarter.uk. For comparison, in Italy the national workplace accident insurer runs a grant call for workplace safety projects that can include a cobot (INAIL notice for the 2025 Italian call). The rules change with each programme period: follow the official pages.

Leasing and rental

With finance leasing the business pays regular instalments instead of the full price up front. For a PoC with a humanoid or quadruped, a 24-36 month lease lets you test the robot on the real process before deciding to scale up. The 40% first-year allowance also covers assets bought for leasing, which concerns the leasing company that buys the robot; how your lease payments are treated is a question for your accountant. With operating rental the asset remains the property of the rental company: ask your accountant whether and how your business can benefit from tax reliefs in that case.

Universities and research centres

Capital allowances and R&D tax relief are aimed at businesses. Universities and public research bodies usually buy with project funds, regional calls or European programmes, each with its own rules on eligible costs (hardware, software, training). In that case the quote has to be built on the categories set by the call. More on this: Universities and Research.

Checklist before ordering

  1. Check what kind of buyer you are: a company (full expensing), a sole trader or partnership (AIA), or an SME manufacturer in England (Made Smarter).
  2. Link the robot to a documented business use, R&D project or adoption project, and choose a version that fits it.
  3. Before ordering, ask your adviser whether HMRC must be notified in advance of an R&D claim, and check availability with your Made Smarter regional partner.
  4. Separate hardware, software and services (training, integration) in the quote.
  5. Involve your accountant or tax adviser before signing the order.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Unitree robot made in China qualify for UK capital allowances?

The descriptions of full expensing, the 40% first-year allowance and the AIA in this overview set no condition on where the asset was made: what counts is whether it is qualifying plant and machinery and, for the 40% allowance, whether it is new and unused. In Italy, the EU production requirement first set for the enhanced depreciation was removed with effect from 1 January 2026. Confirmation for your specific asset is for your accountant or tax adviser.

Are capital allowances a cash grant?

No. They let you deduct the cost of qualifying plant and machinery from your profits before tax. They reduce taxable profit in the period you claim, so they only help a business that has tax to pay. The R&D expenditure credit works differently: it is calculated at 20% of qualifying expenditure on R&D.

Does it apply to leasing too?

The 40% first-year allowance also covers assets bought for leasing, which is relevant to the leasing company that buys the robot. How your lease payments are treated depends on the type of lease: check it with your accountant. With operating rental the asset remains the property of the rental company: check who can benefit from the relief in that case.

Can a university use capital allowances to buy a G1?

In general no: these reliefs are aimed at businesses that pay Corporation Tax or Income Tax. Universities and public research bodies usually buy with project funds, regional calls or European programmes, each with its own spending rules.

Do training and software count?

It depends on the scheme. Where they are available, Made Smarter Adoption match-funded grants can cover hardware or software. The capital allowances described here concern plant and machinery and do not mention training: it is best kept as a separate item in the quote and checked with your accountant.
