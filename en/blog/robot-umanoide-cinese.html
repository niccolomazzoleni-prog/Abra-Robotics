Chinese humanoid robot: who makes it and what reaches the EU

31 October 2026Abra Robotics
In brief: the five Chinese manufacturers to compare are Unitree, UBTech, AgiBot, Fourier and EngineAI. Only for Unitree do we have a verifiable public price list in euros: R1 Air €7,190, G1 Air €19,809, H2 Air €43,874, excluding VAT. To buy in the EU you need CE marking and a declaration of conformity; before ordering, the importer, warranty, spare parts and firmware updates must be put in writing.

A Chinese humanoid robot can be a product with a price list, spare parts and CE marking, or a prototype seen at a trade fair. The country of manufacture matters less than what the supplier puts in writing on conformity, support and updates. Five manufacturers are enough to compare what they declare and what you need to buy in Europe.

General information, not legal advice. A robot's conformity depends on its configuration, intended use and how it is placed on the market: a specific case needs a dedicated assessment.

Where to buy: a product you can purchase or a demo

Before signing, check that the model you are buying is already ready for the European market. Six checks.

  • CE marking and EU declaration of conformity. The CE mark is not a quality badge or an EU approval: it is the manufacturer's declaration that the product complies with the applicable rules. For a machine you need a conformity assessment, a technical file and an EU declaration, as the European Commission explains.
  • Identified importer. The importer places the robot on the EU market and must check that the manufacturer has carried out the assessment, that the EU declaration and technical documentation are available and that contact with the manufacturer is possible.
  • Complete price and delivery. Price in euros, VAT, transport, duty, delivery times and Incoterms written in the offer.
  • Written warranty. Duration, what it covers, who intervenes and how quickly. A "factory" warranty without conditions cannot be verified.
  • Spare parts. Part numbers, availability and lead times. A robot without spare parts is a demo that lasts until the first failure.
  • Firmware and SDK. Who updates it, how often, whether the internet is needed and where the data goes. If the project requires your own code, the version must be the open one.

A unit brought to a trade fair may be a one-off, with demonstration software and without the configuration that will be delivered. The model in the video is not necessarily the one you buy: ask for the serial number, the exact configuration and the declaration of conformity referring to that number.

Abra Robotics supplies the Unitree range in the United Kingdom, Ireland and internationally, with support in English, a public price list and spare parts in the catalogue. For the other brands, the importer and who is responsible for the warranty still need to be verified.

Warranty, spare parts and firmware: the Unitree figures

The official Unitree data sheets give different warranties by model: 6 months for R1 Air, 8 months for R1, 12 months for R1 EDU; 8 months for G1 and 18 months for G1 EDU; 8 months for H2 and 12 months for H2 EDU. These are commercial terms declared by the manufacturer, to be read in the warranty booklet together with the clauses of the sales contract.

The price list includes original Unitree parts for the G1: battery, main control board and foot sole. For a laboratory that uses the robot every day, a failure without a spare part stops work.

On firmware, the R1, G1 and H2 declare OTA updates. Before connecting the robot to the corporate network, it is worth deciding whether updates come over the internet or from an offline package, who has access and which documentation is archived with each version. For the other brands, on the pages consulted the commercial warranty period is not published: it must be requested in writing together with the maintenance plan.

CE and the Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 from 20 January 2027

A humanoid used in a company for a production task may fall within the definition of machinery. Today the reference is Directive 2006/42/EC; from 20 January 2027 Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 becomes mandatory, and it applies directly in all Member States. The European Commission page states that it applies from 20 January 2027.

The regulation adds requirements for safety functions based on artificial intelligence and for the cybersecurity of relevant software, updates the conformity assessment for higher-risk machinery and clarifies when instructions and the declaration can be provided in digital format. The obligations concern manufacturer, importer and distributor, not only whoever designs the robot.

The European Commission states that machinery placed on the market before 20 January 2027 must comply with Directive 2006/42/EC; the regulation applies mandatorily from that date. The directive also applies when an existing machine is modified to the point of becoming a new machine: this is the point to clarify before changing a controller or an end effector.

The European Commission also recalls that voluntary certificates are not a recognised means of demonstrating conformity. For a buyer the practical question is this: does the EU declaration I receive cover the configuration I am buying, with the hands and accessories fitted? If the answer is "we will add it later", the product is not ready for purchase.

Which one to look at first

  • Limited budget and custom software: R1 EDU, or G1 EDU if you need more payload.
  • Adult size and interaction: H2, GR-3 or A2 Ultra. They are different products in price, DoF and target market.
  • Research on locomotion and open code: EngineAI PM01 or T800, if the team wants to start from the low-level interfaces.
  • Repetitive task and high cycle rate: a cobot or a dedicated cell, not a humanoid. The comparison between the Unitree families is in 2026 humanoid comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Which Chinese companies make humanoid robots?

Among the most cited names are Unitree, UBTech, AgiBot, Fourier and EngineAI. Each has its own models and specifications; the official website is the source to check before a quote.

How much does a Chinese humanoid robot cost in Europe?

For Unitree the public price list in euros starts at €7,190 (R1 Air), €19,809 (G1 Air) and €43,874 (H2 Air), excluding VAT. For the other manufacturers we did not find a public European price list.

Can a Chinese humanoid robot be sold in the EU without CE marking?

No, if it is a machine intended for the EU market: CE marking with the EU declaration of conformity is required. A voluntary certificate does not replace the assessment required by law.

What is the difference between a demo robot and one you can buy?

A product you can buy has a price, importer, warranty, spare parts and firmware declared for the market where you buy it. A demo may be a one-off, without these conditions in writing.

What changes with Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 from 20 January 2027?

It replaces Directive 2006/42/EC and becomes mandatory throughout the EU. It adds requirements on AI-based safety functions, cybersecurity and digital instructions, with obligations for manufacturer, importer and distributor.

What is the difference between H2 Air and H2 EDU?

The H2 Air does not include custom software development (SDK) or the dexterous hands; the H2 EDU does. Both declare 31 DoF, a height of 182 cm and 360 N·m at the legs.
