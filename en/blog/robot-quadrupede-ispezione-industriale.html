Inspection quadrupeds: which model for which site

13 July 2026Abra Robotics
In short: for industrial inspection with a quadruped robot, three things matter: IP rating, payload while walking and run time. The Go2 has no stated IP rating, the AS2 (Pro, X, EDU) states IP54, the A2 IP56 (A2 Pro up to IP67 on the main components) and the B2 IP67. None of these platforms is listed as ATEX certified on the official Unitree pages: they must not be used in areas with an explosive atmosphere.

A quadruped robot for industrial inspection is a mobile platform that carries sensors (cameras, thermal cameras, LiDAR, gas detectors) along a repeatable route: substations, machine rooms, warehouses, yards, service tunnels. Compared with a wheeled robot it handles steps, grating and ramps; compared with a humanoid it is more stable and carries more payload for the same weight.

The Unitree range covers four sizes. The choice depends less on the robot's "power" and more on the environment: dust, rain, temperature, stairs, the length of the inspection round and the weight of the sensors to be mounted.

What IP54, IP56 and IP67 mean

The IP code is defined by the IEC 60529 standard. The first digit is the protection against dust, the second against water:

  • IP54: protected against dust (not dust-tight) and against water splashes from any direction. Fine for dusty warehouses and light rain.
  • IP56: the same dust protection, plus resistance to powerful water jets. Suited to yards and outdoor routes in bad weather.
  • IP67: dust-tight and resistant to temporary immersion. This is the level you need for deep puddles, washdowns and very dirty environments.

One detail that matters: on the AS2 the IP54 rating is stated only for the Pro, X and EDU versions, not for the AS2 Air. On the A2 Pro, Unitree specifies that IP67 applies to the main components, while the robot as a whole is rated between IP56 and IP67.

Which model for which site

Go2 EDU: trials, lab work, clean indoor spaces

The Go2 EDU is the starting point for developing and validating an inspection application at low cost: SDK and ROS 2 available, integrated 4D LiDAR L2, 15000 mAh battery. The limit is environmental: Unitree states neither an IP rating nor an operating temperature. The Go2 Air and Pro versions do not allow secondary development; the full comparison is in the article Go2 vs Go2 Pro.

AS2: surveillance and inspection indoors and in light outdoor use

The AS2 weighs about 20 kg and, in the Pro, X and EDU versions, states IP54, a 64-128 line industrial LiDAR, a 15000 mAh (648 Wh) battery and a GPS/4G module, disabled by default and enabled on the user's authorisation. Secondary development is supported on the X and EDU, not on the Pro; only the EDU includes the charging dock. To go further: the Unitree AS2 guide, and the AS2 EDU and AS2 Pro product pages.

A2: semi-outdoor plants and long rounds

The A2 carries about 25 kg in continuous walking and states over 3 hours and about 12.5 km with that load, with a single 9000 mAh or dual 18000 mAh battery. The A2 Pro version has two LiDARs instead of one and includes GPS, 4G and a wireless following module (optional on the standard A2). It is the right size when the sensor package weighs more than an AS2 can carry.

B2: harsh environments, rain, heavy loads

The B2 is the most rugged industrial quadruped in the range: IP67, about 60 kg, over 40 kg of payload while walking, a quick-swap 45 Ah (2250 Wh) battery and optional autonomous charging. Unitree positions it for industrial and electrical plant inspection. Standard sensors (varying by configuration): one 3D LiDAR, two depth cameras and two optical cameras. Unitree notes that some functions require an operator or secondary development.

ATEX: the limit to clear up first

Equipment intended for potentially explosive atmospheres must comply with Directive 2014/34/EU (ATEX). The official pages for the Go2, AS2, A2 and B2 do not state any ATEX or explosion-proof certification. These robots must therefore not be used inside classified zones (refineries, solvent stores, certain areas of chemical and oil and gas plants).

Mounting a gas detector on the robot does not change this: the sensor measures the atmosphere, but the robot remains uncertified equipment. For classified zones you need a platform with ATEX certification stated by the manufacturer. This is general information, not legal advice: zone classification is the responsibility of the employer and its engineers.

Inspection sensors in the price list

The robot is the platform; the result of the inspection depends on the sensors. The Abra Robotics price list already includes some packages for the Go2 / Go2-W family and for the B2:

  • Go2 / Go2-W gas detection sensor (from €2,201 excluding VAT): according to the datasheet it measures oxygen, carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide, methane, hydrogen sulphide, benzene, temperature and humidity, with readings in the Go2 app. There is also the complete Go2 Gas Detection configuration.
  • Remote control with screen and dual-spectrum gimbal (from €14,674 excluding VAT): 3-axis stabilised gimbal with a 640×512 thermal camera in the 8-14 μm band.
  • Additional LiDAR kits for the Go2: Livox Mid-360 and Hesai XT16, useful for denser maps.
  • LiDAR SLAM kit for B2 / B2-W: 32-channel LiDAR and an Intel Core i7 board with basic navigation algorithms.

Third-party sensors (other radiometric thermal cameras, analysers, dedicated radios) can be considered on the AS2 EDU, A2 and B2, but mechanical, electrical and software compatibility must be checked case by case before ordering.

How to choose: six questions

  1. Is the route outdoors or wet? Light rain: AS2 (IP54). Water jets and bad weather: A2. Puddles, washdowns, mud: B2.
  2. Are there ATEX zones? If the route crosses them, none of these models is suitable for those zones.
  3. How much do the sensors weigh? Up to about 8 kg: Go2 EDU. Up to 13-15 kg: AS2. Up to about 25 kg: A2. More: B2.
  4. How long is the round? Compare run time with load, not unladen, and consider automatic charging docks or boards.
  5. Minimum and maximum temperature? The AS2, A2 and B2 state at least -20 to 50 °C; for the Go2 the figure is not stated.
  6. Do the data have to feed into your own software? Then you need a version with secondary development (Go2 EDU, AS2 X or EDU, or the development configurations of the A2 and B2, to be confirmed in the quote).

Frequently asked questions

Which Unitree quadruped is best suited to outdoor inspection?

It depends on water and load. The B2 states IP67 and over 40 kg of walking payload; the A2 states IP56 and about 25 kg; the AS2 in the Pro, X and EDU versions states IP54 and 13-15 kg. The Go2 has no stated IP rating.

Are Unitree quadrupeds ATEX certified?

The official Unitree pages for the Go2, AS2, A2 and B2 do not list any ATEX or explosion-proof certification. They must therefore not be used inside zones with a potentially explosive atmosphere.

How long does the battery of an inspection quadruped last?

According to Unitree, with load: AS2 over 2.5 hours with 13-15 kg, A2 over 3 hours with 25 kg, B2 over 4 hours with 20 kg. The Go2 EDU states 2-4 hours without specifying the load. These are laboratory values.

Can a thermal camera be mounted on a Unitree quadruped?

Yes. For the Go2 and Go2-W the price list includes a remote control with screen and dual-spectrum gimbal with a 640×512 thermal camera (8-14 μm). On the AS2, A2 and B2, third-party sensors must be checked case by case.

How much does an industrial inspection quadruped cost?

In the Abra Robotics price list, excluding VAT: Go2 EDU from €8,804, AS2 EDU from €12,473, AS2 Pro on request, A2 from €30,814, A2 Pro from €42,407, B2 from €66,828. Sensors and accessories are not included.
