Unitree R1 vs G1: which humanoid to choose

16 October 2026Abra Robotics
In brief: the R1 is the entry-level biped: 123 cm, about 29 kg, 20-26 degrees of freedom, about 1 hour of battery life and list prices for the biped starting at €7,190. The G1 is the research platform: 132 cm, about 35 kg, 23-43 degrees of freedom, about 2 hours and knee torque up to 120 N·m. On both, only the EDU version can be programmed. Teaching and first locomotion tests point to the R1 EDU; manipulation and physical AI to the G1 EDU.

If you are searching for unitree r1 vs g1, you are choosing between Unitree's two most affordable bipedal humanoids. The one-line answer: the R1 is the low-cost entry point, the G1 is the platform for serious research. The differences that decide the purchase are price, motor power, degrees of freedom and battery life. Here we put them side by side, using Unitree's official data and the prices from the public price list.

What the R1 does and what the G1 does

The R1 is presented as an "intelligent pioneer": 20-26 degrees of freedom for complex scenarios, a head with 2 degrees of freedom, about 29 kg, ready to use out of the box. It integrates a multimodal model for voice and images (4-microphone array and speaker) and receives OTA updates. The slogan is "movement first, tasks as well": range of movement comes first, tasks follow. Arm payload stays at about 2 kg across all versions.

The G1 is specified with 23-43 motors, imitation learning and reinforcement learning, the Dex3-1 three-finger force-controlled hand (tactile sensors optional) and the unified UnifoLM model. The G1 EDU raises knee torque to 120 N·m and payload per arm to about 3 kg. The R1 walks and interacts; the G1 adds grasping and manipulation.

The deciding difference: SDK and secondary development

On both models the Secondary Development feature is only enabled on the EDU versions. The R1 Air, R1 Basic and base G1 run with the Unitree app or the remote control and do not run your own code.

  • R1 EDU (from R1-U1): secondary development enabled, optional compute module (Orin 40-100 TOPS) and support for the main simulation platforms.
  • G1 EDU (from G1-U1): secondary development enabled, SDK2 in C++ and optional compute module (Orin and others).

How this works in practice is covered in the G1 programming guide. The R1 Air, R1 Basic and base G1 have no SDK: if you need to run your own code, the real starting price is €14,930 (R1-U1) or €38,005 (G1-U1).

When to choose the R1

  • Teaching. Robotics courses, university labs, exercises on locomotion and control. The Air demonstrates movement without being programmed; the EDU runs students' exercises.
  • First reinforcement learning tests. With the EDU you train in simulation and transfer to the robot.
  • Demos, trade fairs and marketing. The Air is inexpensive, walks and draws a crowd.
  • Budget under €20,000. It is the only biped in the price list with a programmable EDU version under €20,000: the R1-U1 costs €14,930.

More on its features and limits: Unitree R1, what it does and what it costs.

When to choose the G1

  • Research on manipulation and physical AI. More torque, more degrees of freedom and the Dex3-1 three-finger hand, with optional tactile sensors.
  • Teleoperation datasets or imitation learning. The higher G1 versions cover the arm, wrist and hand degrees of freedom you need to collect demonstrations.
  • Industrial PoC with object grasping. If the robot also has to walk. If it only has to manipulate at a bench, consider the G1-D, without legs, on a fixed or mobile base.
  • Longer battery life. About 2 hours against the R1's one hour.

Choosing between G1 configurations is explained in which G1 version to choose; for the full picture with the H2, R1-D and G1-D, see the 2026 humanoid comparison.

A rule of thumb for deciding

  • Budget under €20,000 and teaching use: R1, in the EDU version if it needs to be programmed.
  • Research on locomotion and control: R1 EDU or G1 EDU U1, depending on budget.
  • Manipulation, grasping, physical AI: G1 EDU.
  • Tactile hands: G1 EDU (Dex3-1 with optional tactile sensors).

Frequently asked questions

Unitree R1 vs G1: what is the main difference?

The R1 is smaller (123 cm, about 29 kg), has 20-26 degrees of freedom, about 1 hour of battery life and list prices for the biped starting at €7,190. The G1 is taller (132 cm, about 35 kg), has 23-43 degrees of freedom, about 2 hours of battery life, knee torque up to 120 N·m and starts at €19,809. Both can only be programmed in the EDU versions.

How much does the R1 cost compared with the G1?

The R1 biped starts at €7,190 (R1 Air) and the first EDU version (R1-U1) costs €14,930. The G1 starts at €19,809 (G1 Air) and the first EDU (G1-U1) costs €38,005. End-user prices excluding VAT; shipping and insurance are quoted in the offer.

Can the R1 and the G1 be programmed?

Only the EDU versions. The R1 Air, R1 Basic and base G1 do not support secondary development and run with the Unitree app or the remote control. For the SDK and secondary development you need the R1 EDU or the G1 EDU.

R1 or G1 for a university?

For robotics courses and first tests of locomotion and control, the R1 EDU is enough and costs less. For research on manipulation, imitation learning or teleoperation datasets you need the G1 EDU: more torque, more degrees of freedom and more hand options.

Which one lasts longer on a battery?

The G1: about 2 hours with the 9000 mAh battery. The R1 is rated at about 1 hour. In both cases a second battery is almost always needed for a full day of testing.
