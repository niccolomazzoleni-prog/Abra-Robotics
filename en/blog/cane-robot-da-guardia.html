Security robot dog: what it can and cannot do

1 November 2026Abra Robotics
In short: a security robot dog is a quadruped with LiDAR, a camera and connectivity that brings video and data to a person. Teleoperated patrols need an operator, a network and video; autonomous patrols need maps, software and human supervision. It does not replace a security guard, standard versions are not ATEX and, with a camera, it brings data protection obligations.

A security robot dog works a repeatable route: a quadruped with LiDAR, a camera and connectivity that brings images, maps and alerts to a person. It does not decide on its own and does not intervene with anyone. It is there to see and measure what happens along that route, at night or in quiet areas.

Security robot dog: what it can and cannot do

With LiDAR, a camera and accessories it can patrol, stream video, run thermal imaging and detect gas.

What it can doWhat it cannot do
Follow a mapped route and repeat itReplace a security guard or a licensed security company
Carry an HD camera, thermal camera, LiDAR, gas sensor and speakerStop, chase or block a person
Stream video and data to a control roomDetect an intrusion without configuration and checks
Climb steps and move over uneven groundGuarantee 24/7 continuity without recharging and supervision

Teleoperated and autonomous patrols

In teleoperated patrol, an operator drives the robot with an app or remote control and watches the video in real time. It needs a stable network and constant attention. This is the way to start without integration software.

In autonomous patrol, the robot follows a map and a route. The Go2 builds a 3D map with its LiDAR and lets you set a route from the app; a patrol with alerts, verification and escalation, however, needs integration software. Moving autonomously does not mean deciding autonomously: someone has to watch the alerts and decide.

Versions in the same family do not have the same capabilities. Go2 Air and Pro cannot be programmed; the Go2 Edu versions have an SDK and ROS 2. The differences are covered in the Go2 vs Go2 Pro guide. The A2 and B2 support custom software development (SDK).

Video streaming and control-room integration

On the Go2 Pro, X and EDU, the 4G module with eSIM allows remote video and commands through the app; locally there is Wi-Fi 6. Unitree documentation describes WebRTC for video, audio, point cloud and commands. The developer documentation also describes an H264 stream at 720p and 15 Hz over UDP.

For the control room, what matters is who watches, where footage is recorded, for how long and who has access; the manufacturer's app is not a VMS, so gateways or integration are needed.

Sensors useful on a patrol

  • 4D or 3D LiDAR: map, localisation and obstacles; it does not replace visual verification.
  • HD camera: context and detail.
  • Dual-spectrum gimbal: in the remote control with screen for Go2 and Go2-W, a 640×512 thermal sensor in the 8-14 μm band, €14,674 excluding VAT from the price list. Useful for people and heat sources, it is not a certified measurement.
  • Gas sensor: module for Go2 and Go2-W, €2,201 excluding VAT from the price list, measuring oxygen, carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide, methane, hydrogen sulphide and benzene.
  • Searchlight, voice and beacon: 3-in-1 module in the remote control with screen for Go2 and Go2-W (searchlight, voice broadcast, red-blue beacon), €6,603 excluding VAT from the price list.

The declared payload is about 7-8 kg on the Go2 (up to 12 kg on X and EDU), 25 kg on the A2 and over 40 kg on the B2. Full comparison in the quadruped for industrial inspection guide.

Weather. For outdoor use, conditions need checking: deep snow, sand and mud are not declared.

Battery life. Go2: 1-2 hours on Air, Pro and X, 2-4 hours on Go2 Edu versions. A2: over 5 hours and 20 km without payload, over 3 hours and 12.5 km with 25 kg. B2: 4-6 hours, over 5 hours and 20 km without payload, over 4 hours and 15 km with 20 kg. The A2 has hot-swappable batteries; the B2 has a removable battery and optional autonomous charging.

ATEX. Standard versions of the Go2, A2 and B2 do not carry ATEX certification and must not be used in areas where an explosive atmosphere may occur. The B2-Ex is a separate variant, not in the End-User price list. A gas sensor does not make the robot ATEX: it measures the atmosphere, it does not certify the machine.

Privacy and video surveillance: the robot is a mobile camera

A robot dog with a camera records identifiable people: under data protection law, that is personal data. You need defined purposes, areas and angles, minimisation and short retention periods. The privacy notice must be given before the monitored area, with a visible sign.

Where video surveillance rules apply, the principles are the same: defined purpose, short retention, restricted access and a visible notice. As a benchmark, Italy's data protection authority (Garante) sets a maximum of 7 days for urban video surveillance and cites 24 hours as sufficient for a small shop; encryption is required for wireless transmissions. No prior authorisation is needed, but a system that automatically detects unusual behaviour requires a data protection impact assessment where the risk is high.

In practice: a sign, a record of processing activities, short retention, named access and masking of irrelevant areas. Video surveillance must not become remote monitoring of workers.

General information, not legal advice. Privacy assessment and area classification depend on the specific case: involve a consultant before installing the system.

PPE and human supervision

The robot must not be used as protection. Anyone intervening wears the PPE required by the risk assessment: helmet, safety footwear, high-visibility clothing and hearing protection where needed.

You need a route supervisor, a reachable emergency stop, a recovery procedure and a person watching the alerts.

Machinery Regulation from 20 January 2027

From 20 January 2027, Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 replaces Directive 2006/42/EC. The European Commission confirms the date and sets out requirements for safety functions using AI and for the cybersecurity of relevant software.

For a security robot dog, three points matter: an autonomous mobile machine requires a protected zone or detection of people and obstacles; critical software and data must be protected from tampering; anyone who substantially modifies the robot, including through software, may take on the obligations of a manufacturer. A robot placed on the market before 20 January 2027 in conformity with the directive remains governed by the directive.

The checklist is in the 2027 Machinery Regulation guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between teleoperated and autonomous patrol for a security robot dog?

In teleoperated mode the operator drives the robot and sees the video in real time; in autonomous mode the robot follows a map, but alerts and exceptions still go to a person.

Can a security robot dog replace a security guard?

No. It carries sensors and video to a specific point, but it does not intervene with people and does not replace a security service.

Does a security robot dog work in the rain and at night?

It depends on the model: the Go2 does not state an IP rating, the A2 is IP56 and the B2 is IP67. Heavy rain, snow, sand and mud need checking.

How much does a security robot dog cost?

From the Abra Robotics price list, excluding VAT: Go2 Air €2,576, A2 Standard €30,814, B2 €66,828. Spare batteries and accessories are extra.

Do I need a privacy notice for a robot dog with a camera?

Yes. Images are personal data: you need a notice before the monitored area, a defined purpose, short retention and limited access. If the system automatically detects unusual behaviour, a data protection impact assessment may be required.

Are Unitree robot dogs ATEX certified?

Standard versions of the Go2, A2 and B2 do not carry ATEX certification. Unitree has a separate B2-Ex variant, not in the End-User price list, which must not be confused with the standard B2.
