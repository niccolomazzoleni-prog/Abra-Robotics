Which Unitree humanoid to choose in 2026

13 July 2026Updated 9 October 2026Abra Robotics
In brief: R1 is the budget entry point (from €7,190, about 1 hour of battery life). G1 is the most widely used research platform (from €19,809, 23–43 degrees of freedom, about 2 hours). H2 is the only adult-size model (1.82 m, 31 degrees of freedom, about 3 hours, from €43,874). G1-D and R1-D have no legs: they are dual-arm torsos for manipulation and data collection. End-user prices excluding VAT; shipping and insurance are quoted in the offer.

Anyone looking for a Unitree humanoid comparison has usually already decided they want a real robot they can order, with a price list in euros. The question is which one. In this guide we put side by side the five models on the price list of Abra Robotics, which supplies the Unitree range in the UK, Ireland and internationally, with support in English: G1, R1, H2, G1-D and R1-D. The specifications come from the official Unitree pages, the prices from our end-user price list.

G1: the most widely used research platform

The G1 is 1.32 m tall, weighs about 35 kg and carries a 9000 mAh quick-release battery for about 2 hours of use. The base version has 23 degrees of freedom (6 per leg, 5 per arm). The EDU versions go up to 43 degrees of freedom with the three-fingered Dex3-1 hands and optional wrists, raise the maximum knee torque from 90 to 120 N·m and accept an NVIDIA Jetson Orin computing module.

The point not to miss: secondary development (SDK, joint access, ROS 2) is available on the EDU versions. If the robot is for theses, papers or software integration, you start from the G1-U1 upwards. To make sense of the U1–U10 codes there is the guide on which version of the G1 to choose; for software, programming the G1.

R1: the lowest budget

The R1 is 1.23 m tall and weighs between 27 and 29 kg. Unitree sells it in three tiers: Air (20 degrees of freedom, monocular camera), R1 (26 degrees of freedom, binocular camera, motorised waist and head) and EDU (26–40 degrees of freedom, optional hands and Orin module). The declared battery life is about 1 hour for all versions.

Here too secondary development is available only on the EDU, so for a lab the realistic price starts from €14,930 (R1 Edu Standard, R1-U1), not from the €7,190 of the R1 AIR. It is still the cheapest biped humanoid on the price list and suits courses, teaching and first reinforcement-learning experiments.

H2: when you need the adult size

The H2 is 1.82 m tall, weighs about 70 kg and has 31 degrees of freedom: 6 per leg, 7 per arm, 3 at the waist and 2 at the head. Maximum torque reaches 360 N·m on the legs and 120 N·m on the arms. The 15 Ah battery (0.972 kWh) gives about 3 hours. Nominal payload per arm is about 7 kg, with peaks around 15 kg.

It needs space, a test area suited to its weight and a use case that really calls for human scale (interaction with people, workstations designed for an adult). Secondary development is available on the H2 EDU. Further reading: Unitree H2 in the UK.

G1-D and R1-D: torsos for manipulation

The G1-D has two 7-degree-of-freedom arms, a 2-degree-of-freedom waist and a column that adjusts the height between 1260 and 1680 mm. The Standard version sits on a fixed stand (about 2 hours); the mobile one has a differential base up to 1.5 m/s with LiDAR and about 6 hours of battery life. On its own page Unitree calls the mobile version "Ultimate"; in our price list it is the Flagship. With end-effectors the total degrees of freedom range from 19 to 33 depending on the hand fitted.

The R1-D (Unitree presents it as the Dual-Arm Humanoid Robot, models R1-A5, R1-A7, R1-A5-D and R1-A7-D) has 5- or 7-degree-of-freedom arms, 2–4 kg payload per arm and about 1.5 hours of battery life. The versions with the D suffix add a mobile frame and go from 683 to 1600 mm in height.

Without legs, the risk of falling disappears and the robot behaves more like a manipulation cell. It is the sensible choice when the task is picking and placing objects on a bench, not crossing a shop floor.

How to choose: five questions

  1. Does it need to walk, or is manipulation enough? If manipulation is enough, G1-D or R1-D.
  2. Do you need to program it? If so, you need the EDU versions, which cost more than the entry versions.
  3. Which hands do you need? Two-fingered gripper, three-fingered Dex3-1 (with or without tactile sensors) or five-fingered hands: they change price and degrees of freedom.
  4. How long must it run without recharging? From about 1 hour (R1) to about 6 hours (G1-D mobile). A spare battery is almost always needed for a full day of testing.
  5. Where will it work? A classroom, a laboratory or a production area have different safety constraints (see Machinery Regulation 2027).

Simplified decision tree

  • Budget under €20,000 and educational use: R1, EDU version if you need to program it.
  • Research on locomotion, embodied AI, datasets: G1 EDU.
  • Hands with tactile sensors: G1-U4 (tactile Dex3-1) or G1-U8 (Revo 2 Touch).
  • Pick and place on a bench or data collection for teleoperation: G1-D or R1-D.
  • Human-scale interaction: H2.
  • Repetitive high-cadence task: a cobot, not a humanoid (why).

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest Unitree humanoid?

Among the bipeds, the R1 AIR at €7,190 excluding VAT (Unitree 2026 price list); the R1 Basic costs €8,262. To program it, however, you need the EDU version, which starts from €14,930 (R1 Edu Standard, R1-U1).

What is the difference between the G1 and the H2?

The G1 is 1.32 m tall, weighs about 35 kg and has 23–43 degrees of freedom. The H2 is 1.82 m tall, weighs about 70 kg, has 31 degrees of freedom and about 3 hours of battery life against the G1's 2.

How long does a Unitree humanoid battery last?

According to Unitree, about 1 hour for the R1, 2 hours for the G1, 3 hours for the H2, 1.5 hours for the R1-D and up to about 6 hours for the G1-D with mobile base.

Can the G1 Air be programmed?

Secondary development with SDK and ROS 2 is available on the EDU versions of the G1. For research and software integration it is best to start from the G1-U1.

G1-D or biped G1 for pick and place?

For picking and placing objects on a bench the G1-D is the better fit: it has no legs, cannot fall and has a column adjustable between 1260 and 1680 mm. The biped G1 is for when the robot has to move around by walking.
