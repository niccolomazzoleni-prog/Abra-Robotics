Humanoids vs traditional robotics: when a humanoid makes sense and when a cobot or AMR is better

Updated 9 October 2026Abra Robotics
In short: for repetitive, high-volume, already defined tasks, cobots and AMRs remain the most predictable choice: declared precision, continuous power, established standards. A humanoid such as the Unitree G1 (about 2 hours of runtime, 2-3 kg per arm) makes sense for research, data collection and variable tasks in spaces designed for people. Often the most sensible route is to automate the bottleneck first and then run a humanoid PoC on a complementary task.

"Can I replace the cobot on the line with a G1?" Almost always the answer is no. The humanoid works, but it solves a different problem. This guide compares the two families using the specifications declared by the manufacturers, the public list prices and the current state of the safety standards, so you can decide where to start.

What we mean by traditional robotics

  • Cobots: collaborative arms for screwdriving, pick and place, machine tending and palletising. Example: the Fairino FR3, 6 axes, 3 kg nominal payload, 622 mm reach, ±0.02 mm repeatability according to ISO 9283 (manufacturer datasheet). Full range on the cobots page.
  • AMRs: autonomous mobile robots for moving carts, racks and pallets on indoor routes. At list price the range starts with the AutoXing Juno Plus. Overview on the AMR page.
  • Quadrupeds: inspection, surveillance and sensor transport in unstructured environments, with models such as the Go2, AS2 and B2.

What they have in common: defined cycles, established reference standards and KPIs that are easy to measure, such as parts per hour, OEE and cycle time.

What a humanoid brings today (and what it does not)

A biped such as the G1 or the H2 moves through spaces designed for people, can switch tasks within the same day and is the platform on which research on embodied AI, teleoperation and imitation learning is done today. The official G1 specifications help to understand the limits:

  • university laboratories and research projects;

It rarely makes sense to replace an already efficient cell with a humanoid.

Safety and standards

General information, not legal advice.

ISO 10218-1:2025 and ISO 10218-2:2025, published in February 2025, replace the 2011 editions and cover industrial robots, applications and cells. Part 1 now incorporates the requirements for collaborative applications that were previously in the ISO/TS 15066 technical specification.

A biped adds one more problem: it stays upright only thanks to active balance control. If it loses power or goes into a stop it can fall, whereas an AMR or a cobot stops where it is. For this category (industrial mobile robots with actively controlled stability, bipeds and quadrupeds) ISO is working on ISO 25785-1, not yet published.

From 20 January 2027 the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies, replacing the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC. What it means for Unitree robots: Machinery Regulation 2027. In practice, the first tests with a humanoid are carried out in a fenced area, with a fall-protection system (harness or overhead crane) and operators trained in the stop procedures.

When to stay with traditional robotics

  • High-volume tasks with a short cycle.
  • You need a CE-marked machine and fast commissioning.
  • The environment is already structured: conveyors, bays, defined routes.
  • The investment must pay back through measurable savings in the short term.

When to consider a humanoid

  • Tasks not yet formalised, or many different low-volume tasks.
  • R&D project, university, research grant.
  • An environment designed for people, hard to adapt with conveyors or AMRs.
  • A budget dedicated to innovation and data collection, not to replacing labour directly.

There is also a middle ground: the G1-D is a legless dual-arm torso, on a fixed stand (G1-D Standard, from €38,005) or on a mobile base (G1-D Flagship, from €51,944). It removes the risk of a fall and stays close to a manipulation cell. To choose between models: which Unitree humanoid to choose.

The path we usually recommend

  1. First: automate the bottleneck with a cobot or AMR, where the return is easier to measure.
  2. Then: a humanoid PoC on a complementary task (inspection, replenishment, data collection), with KPIs written down before you start.
  3. Scale-up: only if the PoC KPIs hold up (success rate, cycle time, total cost).

For a company purchase, cobots, AMRs and humanoids may qualify for UK schemes that reduce the net cost, from capital allowances to R&D tax relief. Frequently asked questions

Can I replace a cobot on the line with a G1 humanoid?

It almost never pays off. A cobot such as the Fairino FR3 declares a repeatability of ±0.02 mm and runs on mains power; the G1 has a declared runtime of about 2 hours and a payload of about 2-3 kg per arm, and Unitree does not declare a repeatability value. The humanoid makes sense on different tasks, not as a replacement for an already efficient cell.

How much does a humanoid cost compared with a cobot?

At the Abra Robotics list price, excluding VAT: Fairino FR3 cobot from €5,448, AutoXing Juno Plus AMR from €16,999, Unitree G1 Air from €18,156, G1 EDU (U1) from €36,826, H2 Air from €43,874. On top of the humanoid's price, add software development, a test area and training.

Is there a dedicated safety standard for humanoids?

Not yet published. ISO 10218-1 and 10218-2 of 2025 cover industrial robots and cells, including collaborative applications. For mobile robots that stay balanced through active control, such as bipeds and quadrupeds, ISO is developing ISO 25785-1. From 20 January 2027 the EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 applies.

When does a humanoid make sense in a company?

When the value lies in flexibility or research: tasks not yet formalised, data collection to train models, environments designed for people that are hard to adapt, R&D projects with a dedicated budget. If the task is repetitive and high-volume, a cobot or an AMR remains the more predictable choice.

Is there a middle ground between a cobot and a humanoid?

Yes: the G1-D is a legless dual-arm Unitree torso, on a fixed stand (Standard, from €38,005) or on a mobile base (Flagship, from €51,944). It removes the biped's risk of falling and stays closer to a manipulation cell.
