Unitree R1: what it really does and what it costs

15 October 2026Abra Robotics
In short: the Unitree R1 is a compact bipedal humanoid, 123 cm tall and weighing about 27 to 29 kg. It walks, moves through complex scenarios and, only in the EDU version, can be programmed (secondary development and simulation). It is not a work robot: it lifts about 2 kg per arm and runs for about 1 hour. The R1 family on the price list goes from €6,295 (R1-A5 Standard) to €35,217 (R1 Edu Professional B), excluding VAT.

Anyone who searches Google for what does the unitree r1 do usually wants to know whether this robot is useful for anything concrete or whether it is a trade-show piece. The answer depends on the project: the R1 does a few things, but it does them well and at a low entry price for a humanoid. Here you will find the functions documented by Unitree, the honest limits and the prices from the price list. All versions are gathered on the R1 range page.

What the Unitree R1 really does

The R1 is a bipedal humanoid 1230 mm tall (1230×357×190 mm standing) and weighs about 27 kg in the Air and about 29 kg in the R1 and EDU versions. It has 20 to 26 degrees of freedom: 6 per leg, 4 or 5 per arm, plus a 2-DoF waist and head on the non-Air versions. The functions documented on the official Unitree page are four.

  • Agile mobility. Unitree describes the 20 to 26 degrees of freedom as the ability to adapt to complex scenarios, with the 2-DoF head improving perception of the surroundings.
  • Multimodal model for voice and images. The robot integrates a large multimodal model, with a 4-array microphone and a speaker, for voice and visual interaction.
  • Open interfaces. The page mentions open control interfaces for joints and sensors and support for the main simulation platforms. Note: this openness applies only to the EDU version, the only one listed with Secondary Development.
  • OTA updates and control. It is controlled with the Unitree app or a remote controller and is updated remotely over the air.

In practice: it walks and responds to commands from the app or the remote controller. Unitree sums it up as "movement first, tasks as well": the variety of movement is the base on which tasks are then completed.

What it does not do: the real limits

The maximum arm payload is about 2 kg, and Unitree points out that it varies a lot with the arm posture. It is not a robot for picking up and moving boxes: for bench manipulation there is the dual-arm R1-D torso.

  • Runtime. About 1 hour on every version: a day of trials almost always needs a second battery.
  • No custom code on Air and Basic. Secondary development is EDU-only. With the Air and Basic you run Unitree's software, not your own applications.
  • Shorter warranty. 6 months on the Air, 8 on the Basic, 12 on the EDU.
  • Manufacturer's warnings. In the notes of the spec sheet, Unitree writes that the humanoid sector is at an early exploration stage, that some of the functions shown are still in development and that a safe distance from the robot must be kept.

The differences come from the parameter table on the official spec sheet.

The arm payload stays at about 2 kg on all three. The choice comes down to one question: do you need to run your own code on it? If so, the EDU is the only option, and the real price starts from €14,930, not from the Air's €7,190.

Who it makes sense for

  • Teaching. Robotics courses and university labs: the Air demonstrates the movements without needing to be programmed; the EDU is for running the students' exercises.
  • Locomotion and reinforcement learning research. With the EDU you train in simulation and transfer to the robot. Check the state of the SDK on the R1 before ordering: see the ROS 2 Unitree guide.
  • Demos, trade shows and communication. The Air is the right version: it is cheap, it walks and it draws attention.

If the task is handling objects on a bench, a biped is not the right tool: consider the R1-D torso. R1 or G1?

The R1 costs far less than the G1 and is smaller and lighter: it is the choice for teaching, labs and first locomotion trials. The G1 has more degrees of freedom in the higher versions and more hand options: it is the platform for research on manipulation and physical AI. The point-by-point comparison is in the 2026 humanoid comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Unitree R1 do?

It walks and moves through complex scenarios; it integrates a multimodal model for voice and images and is updated over the air. The Air and Basic are used with the Unitree app or a remote controller; the EDU version can be programmed, with secondary development and simulation. The arm payload is about 2 kg.

How much does the Unitree R1 cost?

End-user prices excluding VAT start from €7,190 (R1 Air) and go up to €35,217 (R1 Edu Professional B). The R1 family on the price list goes from €6,295 (R1-A5 Standard torso) to €35,217. Unitree lists $4,900 for the Air and $5,900 for the R1.

Can the R1 Air be programmed?

No. Secondary development is available only on the EDU version. The Air and Basic are used with the Unitree app or a remote controller; to run your own code you need an R1 EDU.

What is the difference between the R1 Air and the R1 EDU?

The R1 Air has 20 degrees of freedom, a monocular camera, no motorised waist or head and a 6-month warranty. The R1 EDU has 26 degrees of freedom (up to 40 with optional hands), a binocular camera, secondary development and a 12-month warranty.

R1 or G1?

The R1 is smaller, lighter and costs far less than the G1: it suits teaching and first locomotion trials. The G1 has more degrees of freedom in the higher versions and more hand options: it is the platform for research on manipulation and physical AI.
