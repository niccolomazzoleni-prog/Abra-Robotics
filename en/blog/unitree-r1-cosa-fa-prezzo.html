Unitree R1: what it does and its price

15 October 2026Abra Robotics
In brief: the Unitree R1 is a compact bipedal humanoid, 123 cm tall and weighing around 27-29 kg. It walks, moves in complex scenarios and, only in the EDU version, can be programmed (secondary development and simulation). It is not a work robot: it lifts about 2 kg per arm and lasts about 1 hour. The R1 family on the price list ranges from €6,294.96 (R1-A5 Standard) to €35,216.56 (R1 Edu Professional B), excluding VAT.

People who search Google for unitree r1 what it does usually want to know whether this robot is good for anything concrete or is just a trade-show object. The answer depends on the project: the R1 does few things, but it does them well, and at a low entry price for a humanoid. Here you will find the functions documented by Unitree, the honest limits and the prices from the list. All versions are collected in the R1 range page.

What the Unitree R1 really does

The R1 is a bipedal humanoid, 1230 mm tall (1230×357×190 mm when standing) and weighing about 27 kg in the Air and about 29 kg in the R1 and EDU versions. It has 20 to 26 degrees of freedom: 6 per leg, 4 or 5 per arm, plus a waist and head with 2 degrees of freedom in the non-Air versions. There are four functions documented on the official Unitree page.

  • Agile mobility. Unitree describes the 20-26 degrees of freedom as the ability to adapt to complex scenarios, with the 2-degree-of-freedom head improving awareness of the environment.
  • Multimodal model for voice and images. The robot integrates a large multimodal model, with a 4-array microphone and speaker, for voice and visual interaction.
  • Open interfaces. The page mentions open control interfaces for joints and sensors and support for the main simulation platforms. Note: this openness applies only to the EDU version, the only one with the Secondary Development entry.
  • OTA updates and control. It is controlled with the Unitree app or remote and updated remotely via software.

In practice: it walks and responds to commands from the app or the remote. Unitree sums it up as "movement first, tasks as well": the variety of movement is the basis for then completing tasks.

What it does not do: the real limits

The maximum payload per arm is about 2 kg and Unitree notes that it varies a lot with the arm posture. It is not a robot for picking up and moving boxes: for bench manipulation there is the two-arm R1-D torso.

  • Battery life. About 1 hour for all versions: a day of testing almost always requires a second battery.
  • No custom code on Air and Basic. Secondary development is only for the EDU. With the Air and Basic you run Unitree software, not your own applications.
  • Limited warranty. 6 months on the Air, 8 on the Basic, 12 on the EDU.
  • Manufacturer's warnings. In the notes of the spec sheet, Unitree writes that the humanoid sector is in an early exploration phase, that some of the functions shown are still in development and that a safe distance from the robot must be kept.

The arm payload stays at about 2 kg on all three. The choice comes down to one question: do you need to run your own code? If so, the only option is the EDU, and the real price starts at €14,929.58, not the €7,190.05 of the Air.

Who it makes sense for

  • Teaching. Robotics courses and university labs: the Air shows the movements without needing to be programmed; the EDU is for running students' exercises.
  • Research on locomotion and reinforcement learning. With the EDU you train in simulation and transfer to the robot. The state of the SDK on the R1 should be checked before ordering: see the ROS 2 Unitree guide.
  • Demos, trade shows and communication. The Air is the right version: it costs little, walks and makes an impression.

If the task is manipulating objects on a bench, a biped is not the right tool: consider the R1-D torso. Before the quote we run a free eligibility check for funding and leasing; for UK customers the schemes are described in the incentives section of the country page.

R1 or G1?

The R1 costs far less than the G1 and is smaller and lighter: it is the choice for teaching, labs and first locomotion tests. The G1 has more degrees of freedom in the higher versions and more hand options: it is the platform for research on manipulation and physical AI. The point-by-point comparison is in the humanoid comparison 2026 article.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Unitree R1 do?

It walks and moves in complex scenarios; it integrates a multimodal model for voice and images and updates over the air. Air and Basic are used with the Unitree app or remote; the EDU version can be programmed with secondary development and simulation. The arm payload is about 2 kg.

How much does the Unitree R1 cost?

End-user prices excluding VAT start at €7,190.05 (R1 Air) and go up to €35,216.56 (R1 Edu Professional B). The R1 family on the price list ranges from €6,294.96 (R1-A5 Standard torso) to €35,216.56. Unitree lists $4,900 for the Air and $5,900 for the R1.

Can the R1 Air be programmed?

No. Secondary development is only available on the EDU version. Air and Basic are used with the Unitree app or remote; for your own code you need an R1 EDU.

What is the difference between the R1 Air and the R1 EDU?

The R1 Air has 20 degrees of freedom, a monocular camera, no motorised waist and head, and a 6-month warranty. The R1 EDU has 26 degrees of freedom (up to 40 with optional hands), a binocular camera, secondary development and a 12-month warranty.

R1 or G1?

The R1 is smaller, lighter and costs far less than the G1: it suits teaching and first locomotion tests. The G1 has more degrees of freedom in the higher versions and more hand options: it is the platform for research on manipulation and physical AI.
