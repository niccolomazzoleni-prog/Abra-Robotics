Unitree G1 · Operating guide

Unitree G1: how to power on, use the remote and connect the PC

Updated 19 October 2026Abra Robotics~20 min

Operating guide for the Unitree G1 EDU: variants, power on, R3 remote commands, battery, network and SSH. The commands follow the official sticker on the remote and the Unitree developer documentation.

Physical protection of the robot

Recommended covers: for demos with movement on the ground, protect the arms and legs with soft covers, for example neoprene. At the first power-on, use the protection frame from the price list.

R3 remote control

The G1 is driven with the Unitree R3 remote, which works with the whole Unitree range. Unitree states a 780 mAh battery for about 5 hours of use (R3 page). To switch it on, according to the G1 user manual: a short press on the power button, then a long press of more than 2 seconds until it beeps.

Remote battery: keep it charged. Without the R3 there is no quick way to stop the robot.

A spare remote is on the price list: G1 Remote Controller.

Remote commands (current firmware)

The combinations can change with the firmware version: always check the sticker on your remote before using the robot.

Command legend printed on the R3 remote:

  • ★ = single short press
  • ▲ = long press of 2 seconds
  • In motion control, holding damping (L2 + B) for 5 seconds enters damping protection. This is the emergency stop described in the manual: the robot lowers itself slowly to the ground.

Safety: to stop the robot use damping, L2 + B (in motion control hold it for 5 seconds for damping protection). Do not confuse it with L2 + Y (zero moment): that zeroes the motor torque and, if the robot is not hanging from the harness or the frame, it falls.

Debug mode (SDK development only)

L2 + R2 stops the motion control program, so it does not conflict with the SDK commands. With L2 + A you check that you are in debug mode; if the robot does not respond as expected, Unitree says to repeat L2 + R2 (Unitree, remote control). You only need it in the lab, with the robot suspended, when commanding the motors at low level. In normal use with the remote you do not enter debug mode.

Recommended power-on sequences (from the sticker)

  • From sitting (recommended): Power on → (1) Damping → (2) Locked standing → (3) Running/Regular → demo → (4) Sitting → power off. Steps (2) and (3) with manual support at the shoulders.
  • From lying down: Power on → (1) → (10) → demo → (6) → power off. Step (10) is not numbered in the sticker table: check it against the delivery documentation.
  • From squat: Power on → (1) → (6) → demo → (6) → power off.

Powering on the G1 battery

  1. Insert the battery from the side of the robot, with the switch facing the rear, until it clicks.
  2. Short press on the power button, then a long press of at least 2 s.
  3. Wait about 1 minute: at the end of start-up all joints are in zero torque.
  4. Follow the remote sequence: first damping (L2 + B), then the other steps.

Suspended on the frame (lab)

Recommended at the first power-on, with the protection frame. Suspend the robot and put the feet on the ground only after it has stabilised in Regular mode. Arms and legs free, no cables under tension.

Ground method (L2+X)

Only on a flat, hard surface. The robot gets up by itself: keep your distance.

Payload and arms

Unitree states a maximum load per arm of about 3 kg on the G1 EDU (about 2 kg on the base G1) and notes that it varies a lot with arm posture (G1 page).

Battery and runtime

According to Unitree, the G1 carries a 9000 mAh quick-release smart battery for about 2 hours of runtime. The real figure drops with continuous walking and loads on the arms.

  • For a full day of tests you need at least two batteries: the extra G1 battery is listed at €880 and the charger at €147 (excluding VAT).
  • Switch the robot off before swapping the battery and put it back in damping before letting it stand up.

Network and SSH

Put the PC on the 192.168.123.0/24 subnet with a free static IP: Unitree suggests 192.168.123.99. Do not use .161, .164 or .120. PC1 (.161) runs the Unitree motion control and is not open to developers; development is done on PC2 (.164).

  • Direct Ethernet to the G1 LAN port (recommended by Unitree)
  • Wi‑Fi: according to the LeRobot documentation, Wi‑Fi on PC2 is disabled by default and must be enabled by hand; to install packages you can share the PC's connection over Ethernet.

Common problems

  • Ping does not respond: check the Ethernet cable and the PC's IP, then restart the robot.
  • The joints shake under SDK control: the motion controller is still running, or the remote and the SDK are sending commands at the same time. For low-level control, enter debug mode; otherwise stop the SDK commands.

FAQ

G1-U1 or G1-U2?

The U1 is enough for locomotion and control. The U2 adds a 3-axis waist and 7-axis arms: you need it if the project involves manipulation.

SSH password?

The default PC2 credentials are unitree / 123. If they do not work, Unitree provides them through support. Change them at the first login.

How do you stop the G1 safely?

With damping, L2 + B. L2 + Y (zero moment) zeroes the torque: a standing robot that is not suspended falls.

How long does the G1 battery last?

About 2 hours according to Unitree. For a full day of tests you need at least two batteries.

Does the G1 Air connect via SSH like the EDU?

No. The PC2 for user code and SDK development are only available on the G1 EDU.
