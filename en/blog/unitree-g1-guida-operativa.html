Unitree G1 · Operating guide

Unitree G1: how to power on, use the remote and connect the PC

Updated 15 October 2026Abra Robotics~20 min

Operating guide for the Unitree G1 EDU: variants, power on, R3 remote commands, battery, network and SSH. The commands follow the official sticker on the remote and the Unitree developer documentation. For programming with the SDK and ROS 2, see the dedicated article.

Physical protection of the robot

Recommended covers: on hard flooring the G1 gets scuffed quickly, especially in demos with a lot of movement on the ground. We recommend soft covers (neoprene, for example) on the arms and legs and, at the first power-on, the protection frame from the price list. The "from the ground" method (L2+X) increases the risk of marks on the elbows and knees.

R3 remote control

Power on: short press on the power button → hold for ≥2 s (two beeps). Power off: short press + long press ≥2 s (three beeps).

Remote battery: it can run flat before the G1 batteries. Keep the remote charged: without the R3 you cannot control the robot safely.

The remote is included or can be bought separately: G1 Remote Controller.

Remote commands (current firmware)

The combinations can change with the firmware version: always check the sticker on your remote before using the robot.

Command legend printed on the R3 remote:

  • ★ = single short press
  • ▲ = long press, about 2 seconds (in motion control, holding the damping combination L2 + B for 5 seconds enters damping protection, as shown on the sticker)

Safety: to stop the robot use damping, L2 + B (in motion control hold it for 5 seconds for damping protection). Do not confuse it with L2 + Y (zero moment): that zeroes the motor torque and, if the robot is not hanging from the harness or the frame, it falls.

Greetings and gestures (handshake, wave, kiss, clap): use them in Regular mode: R1 + Y if you have a 3 DoF waist (U2+) or R1 + X with a 1 DoF waist (U1). In Running mode (R2+A) the robot is less stable, and a handshake or wave can unbalance it. Interactive commands: SELECT + A (handshake), SELECT + Y (wave), double click Y/X/A (see the sticker).

Debug mode (SDK development only)

L2 + R2 stops the motion controller to avoid conflicts with the SDK; with L2 + A you check that you are in debug mode (source: Unitree, remote control). You only need it in the lab when programming the motors at low level. For normal use with the remote, do not enter debug mode: restart the G1 to exit.

Recommended power-on sequences (from the sticker)

  • From sitting (recommended): Power on → (1) Damping → (2) Locked standing → (3) Running/Regular → demo → (4) Sitting → power off. Steps (2) and (3) with manual support at the shoulders.
  • From lying down: Power on → (1) → (10) → demo → (6) → power off.
  • From squat: Power on → (1) → (6) → demo → (6) → power off.

Powering on the G1 battery

  1. Insert the battery until it clicks (switch towards the rear).
  2. Short press on the button → hold for ≥2 s.
  3. Wait about 1 min: all joints in zero torque.
  4. Follow the remote sequence above: first damping (L2 + B), then proceed.

Suspended on the frame (lab)

Recommended at the first power-on: protection frame. Suspend the robot, and put the feet on the ground only after it has stabilised in Regular mode. Arms and legs free, no cables under tension.

Ground method (L2+X)

Only on a flat, hard surface. The robot gets up by itself: keep your distance. With manipulators (U3–U10) avoid this method. Use neoprene covers: movement on concrete marks plastic and aluminium straight away.

Payload and arms

Unitree states a maximum load per arm of about 3 kg on the G1 EDU (about 2 kg on the base G1) and notes that it varies a lot with arm posture (G1 page). In practice, with the arm extended even 1 kg can tip the robot forward and disturb walking. For greetings and gestures, do not load the arms.

Battery and runtime

According to Unitree, the G1 carries a 9000 mAh quick-release smart battery for about 2 hours of runtime. The real figure drops with continuous walking and loads on the arms.

  • For a full day of tests you need at least two batteries: the extra G1 battery is listed at €880 and the charger at €147 (excluding VAT).
  • Switch the robot off before swapping the battery and put it back in damping before letting it stand up.
  • Keep the R3 remote charged too: without the remote you have no quick way to stop the robot.

Network and SSH

Put the PC on the 192.168.123.0/24 subnet with a free static IP (e.g. .51; do not use .161, .164, .120). PC1 (.161) runs the Unitree motion control and is not open to developers; development is done on PC2 (.164).

  • Direct Ethernet to the G1 LAN port
  • Wi‑Fi: according to the LeRobot documentation, Wi‑Fi on PC2 is disabled by default and must be enabled by hand; to install packages you can share the PC's connection over Ethernet.

Credentials: unitree / 123 are the defaults, also reported in the LeRobot documentation. Unitree says to ask support for the initial credentials, so on some units they may differ. Change the password after the first login, especially if the robot joins the company network.

H2: uses the same 192.168.123.x subnet, with a PC1 for control (Intel Core i5) and a PC2 for development (Intel Core i7 on the EDU version, according to the Unitree page). The exact addresses depend on the configuration: check them in the delivery documentation.

Common problems

  • Ping does not respond → check the Ethernet cable and the PC's IP, then restart the robot.
  • SSH does not connect / is slow → too many SSH sessions or the Jetson is saturated → restart the G1.
  • The SDK makes the joints shake → motion controller running in parallel, or double command (remote + SDK at the same time). Enter debug mode only for development, or stop the SDK commands.

FAQ

G1-U1 or G1-U2?

U1 = 23 DoF, 1 DoF waist. U2 = 29 DoF, 3 DoF waist and 7 DoF arms, the base for manipulation.

Which walk mode for greetings?

R1+Y (3 DoF waist, U2+) or R1+X (1 DoF waist, U1), in Regular mode, not R2+A Running mode.

SSH password?

Default unitree / 123 on 192.168.123.164 (PC2). On some units they may differ: Unitree provides them through support. Change them after the first login.

How do you stop the G1 safely?

With damping, L2 + B. Do not use L2 + Y (zero moment): it zeroes the torque and the robot falls if it is not suspended.

How long does the G1 battery last?

About 2 hours according to Unitree, with the 9000 mAh battery. For a full day of tests you need at least two batteries.

Does the G1 Air connect via SSH like the EDU?

No. Secondary development is only available on the G1 EDU, which has the PC2 for user code.
