Unitree robots and the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230: what changes for buyers from 20 January 2027

1 October 2026Updated 15 October 2026Abra Robotics~9 min

From 20 January 2027, machinery sold in the European Union falls under Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, which replaces the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC. Humanoids such as the G1 or the H2 and quadrupeds such as the Go2 or the B2, used in a business, are machinery: this guide explains what changes for buyers, who signs what, and what to check before putting them on the shop floor.

General information, not legal advice. How a robot is classified, and the obligations that follow, depend on the actual application, the software installed and how the robot fits into the process. A specific case needs a dedicated assessment.

In brief

  • The regulation applies from 20 January 2027 and is directly valid in every Member State, with no national transposition.
  • A robot placed on the market in conformity with the directive before that date can still be sold (art. 52).
  • Anyone who substantially modifies a robot, even through software alone, becomes the manufacturer of that machine (art. 18).
  • Explicit requirements come in on safety software, protection against tampering and autonomous mobile machinery (Annex III).
  • A technical standard dedicated to humanoids and quadrupeds (ISO 25785-1) is still under development.

What the Machinery Regulation is and when it applies

Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 sets the safety requirements a machine must meet when it is placed on the market or put into service in the EU. Because it is a regulation and not a directive, it applies in exactly the same way in every Member State.

The date of application is 20 January 2027, as reported by the European Agency for Safety and Health at Work (EU-OSHA). The original text gave 14 January; the date was corrected by a corrigendum published in July 2023. Some parts already apply, for example the rules on notified bodies.

For robots already in circulation, article 52 is what matters: Member States cannot impede the making available of products placed on the market in conformity with Directive 2006/42/EC before the regulation applies. A G1 or a Go2 sold today with documentation that conforms to the directive does not need to be "redone" in 2027.

Who the manufacturer is and who signs the declaration

The EU declaration of conformity and the CE marking are the responsibility of the manufacturer (art. 10). The regulation, however, identifies other parties who can take on that role:

  • Importer (art. 13): before placing the robot on the market, checks that the manufacturer has carried out the conformity assessment, drawn up the technical documentation and affixed the CE marking.
  • Distributor (art. 15): must exercise due care regarding conformity.
  • An importer or distributor selling under its own brand, or modifying the product in a way that affects conformity, becomes the manufacturer (art. 17).
  • Anyone who makes a substantial modification becomes the manufacturer of that machine (art. 18).

Anyone who assembles several machines that work together as a whole for the same result also creates an "assembly of machinery" (art. 3), which is itself a machine with its own manufacturer. This is the typical case of a robot integrated into a cell with conveyors, guards and a safety PLC.

When a modification is substantial

The definition in article 3, point 16, is precise. A modification is substantial when, by physical or digital means, after the product is placed on the market, it:

  1. is not foreseen or planned by the manufacturer;
  2. affects safety by creating a new hazard or increasing an existing risk;
  3. requires new guards or protective devices that involve changing the safety control system, or new measures for stability or mechanical strength.

For a Unitree robot, the cases to assess carefully are:

  • a locomotion controller written or trained by you in place of the factory one;
  • an end effector or a payload that shifts the centre of gravity and stability;
  • use in an area shared with people that the manufacturer had not foreseen.

Repair and maintenance that do not affect conformity are not substantial modifications (recital 26 of the regulation). A non-professional user who modifies their own machine for their own use does not become a manufacturer. A company or a laboratory, on the other hand, is a professional user.

What changes compared with Directive 2006/42/EC

Software and digital components

The regulation defines a "safety component" to include a digital component, including software, placed on the market separately (art. 3, point 3). Software changes are expressly among those that can be substantial.

Protection against tampering (Annex III, point 1.1.9)

  • Connecting another device, including a remote one, must not create hazardous situations.
  • Safety-critical software and data must be identified and protected against accidental or intentional alteration.
  • The machine must be able to identify the safety software installed and keep evidence of interventions, legitimate or not.

Control systems (Annex III, point 1.2.1)

Control systems must also withstand reasonably foreseeable deliberate attempts by third parties. Versions of safety software loaded after the machine is placed on the market must be traceable for five years. For systems with self-evolving behaviour:

  • they must not perform actions beyond the defined task and movement space;
  • they must record data on safety decisions, to be kept for one year;
  • it must be possible to correct them at any time.

Autonomous mobile machinery (Annex III, part 3)

A quadruped that patrols an area autonomously (for example an AS2 or an A2 on inspection) can fall within the definition of autonomous mobile machinery. Two options are provided, used singly or combined depending on the risk assessment:

  • working in a zone enclosed by perimeter guarding;
  • having devices that detect people and obstacles nearby.

Where needed, a remote supervision function must be provided, limited to stopping, starting and moving to a safe position.

Digital instructions (art. 10, para. 7)

Instructions can be supplied in digital form, downloadable and accessible for at least 10 years. On request at the time of purchase, the manufacturer provides them free of charge in paper form within one month.

Notified body

The categories in Annex I, part A, require a procedure involving a notified body (art. 25, para. 2). These include machinery that integrates systems with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour, based on machine learning, that ensure safety functions, limited to those systems. A controller that has been trained and then frozen is not automatically a self-evolving system: the classification has to be made on the specific case.

AI Act and Cyber Resilience Act

  • AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). An AI system acting as a safety component of a machine can be high-risk (art. 6, para. 1). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI), published in the Official Journal of the EU on 24 July 2026, moved the Machinery Regulation to section B of Annex I of the AI Act. In practice, the AI Act requirements for AI embedded in machinery will be brought into the Machinery Regulation through delegated acts, which the Commission must adopt by 2 August 2028 (Cuatrecasas summary). The delegated acts have not yet been issued: the framework should be rechecked at the time of purchase.
  • Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847). It covers connected products with digital elements. According to the European Commission, the reporting obligations apply from 11 September 2026 and the main obligations from 11 December 2027.

Reference technical standards

  • ISO 12100: general principles for risk assessment and risk reduction.
  • ISO 10218-1 and ISO 10218-2 (2025 edition): safety of industrial robots and their applications.
  • ISO/TS 15066: human-robot collaboration.
  • ISO 13482: personal care robots.
  • IEC 62443: cybersecurity of industrial automation systems.

There is still no dedicated standard for humanoids and quadrupeds. ISO 25785-1, on industrial mobile robots with actively controlled stability (legged, wheeled or other forms of locomotion), is under development.

Checklist: before putting a Unitree robot on the shop floor

This already applies today. As an employer, UK health and safety law already requires you to make suitable equipment available and to assess the risks of its use; in Italy the equivalent rule is Legislative Decree 81/2008 (arts. 70 and 71). The regulation adds attention to software and connectivity.
  1. Documents for the exact model. Ask for the EU declaration of conformity, instructions in English and the reference firmware version. Check that the model and variant match the robot delivered.
  2. Intended use. Compare the application you have in mind with the intended use stated in the instructions. If you go beyond it, you are in substantial modification territory.
  3. Risk assessment to ISO 12100. You need to cover:
    • the robot falling and losing balance;
    • collisions with people;
    • loads in the hands or on the back;
    • batteries and charging;
    • behaviour when communication is lost.
  4. Working area. Decide whether the robot works in a segregated or a shared zone. In a shared zone you need people detection and speed limits that are verified, not just declared.
  5. Stopping. Check where the emergency stop and the remote control are, how they work, and who holds them during operation.
  6. Software. Keep a log of installed versions and updates. Any controller you write yourself must be assessed before use near people.
  7. Network and cybersecurity. On a plant network, apply three measures:
    • put the robot on a separate VLAN;
    • change the default credentials;
    • disable the remote services you do not use and decide who can update the firmware.
  8. Assembly of machinery. If the robot goes into a cell with other machines, establish in writing who is the manufacturer of the assembly and who signs the declaration.
  9. Training. Train the people who work near the robot, not just those who program it.

Universities and laboratories

Article 2, paragraph 2, point (m), excludes from the regulation machinery "specifically designed and constructed for research purposes for temporary use in laboratories". A commercial robot bought from a catalogue does not automatically fall within this exclusion, because it was created as a product for sale. For a laboratory, the most prudent route is still to treat it as a machine: risk assessment, dedicated area and software log. More for research groups on our Universities and research page.

FAQ: Machinery Regulation and Unitree robots

From when does the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 apply?

From 20 January 2027. From that date it replaces Directive 2006/42/EC. Some parts, such as the rules on notified bodies, already apply.

Does a Unitree robot bought in 2026 need to be made compliant again in 2027?

No, if it was placed on the market in conformity with Directive 2006/42/EC before the regulation applies (art. 52). The employer remains responsible for safe use in the workplace.

If I write my own software for a G1 or a Go2, do I become the manufacturer?

Only if the modification is substantial: not foreseen by the manufacturer, creating a new hazard or increasing a risk, and requiring new guards, a change to the safety control system or new stability measures. An application that stays within the intended limits usually is not, but it has to be assessed case by case.

Do humanoids have to go through a notified body?

Not simply because they are humanoids. A notified body is required for the categories in Annex I, part A, which include self-evolving systems based on machine learning that ensure safety functions.

Is there a specific technical standard for humanoids and quadrupeds?

Not yet published. ISO 25785-1 is under development. Today ISO 12100 is used for risk assessment and the ISO 10218 series where applicable.

Does a robot with artificial intelligence also fall under the AI Act?

It can, if the AI performs a safety function. With Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 the AI Act requirements for AI embedded in machinery will be inserted into the Machinery Regulation through delegated acts, to be adopted by 2 August 2028.

Are universities excluded from the Machinery Regulation?

The exclusion covers machinery designed and built specifically for research and used temporarily in a laboratory. A commercial robot from a catalogue does not automatically qualify: it has to be checked case by case.
